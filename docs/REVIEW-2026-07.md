# Hearthmind architecture review — v0.39.0 (July 2026)

An independent-review-board pass over the whole repository, with no
attachment to prior design decisions. Every empirical claim below was
measured in this environment (real engine, LLM disabled → deterministic
fallbacks, in-memory DB) — probe scripts and raw numbers are summarized
inline. Where a finding contradicts the project's own self-description,
the contradiction is stated plainly.

Severity scale: **Critical** (will break a long-running world or
invalidates a core claim), **High** (compounds across releases),
**Medium** (real but contained), **Low** (note for later).

---

## 0. The two commissioned questions first

### 0.1 Why does population collapse?

**Answer: collapse is an early-game winter funnel, not a long-run
failure — and the long-run attractor is the opposite problem
(runaway growth pinned at POPULATION_CAP).**

Measured (engine runs, fallback cognition, 30k ticks):

| seed | t=2,000 | t=6,000 | t=22,000 | t=30,000 | starvation deaths |
|------|---------|---------|----------|----------|-------------------|
| 42   | 10      | 20      | 200 (cap)| 200      | 34                |
| 7    | **7**   | **8**   | 174      | 200      | 12                |

- Worlds begin at tick 0 = January 1 = **deep winter**: resource regen
  ×0.3, farm growth ×0.35, harsh-weather hunger ×1.3 — with 12 agents
  scattered randomly across a 64×64 map, no farms, no granary, no
  roads. Nearly all starvation deaths cluster in this window (seed 7
  lost 5 of 12 by tick 2,000 and sat at 7–8 people for ~8,000 ticks).
- The outcome is **bimodal**: survive the first winter and the world
  grows monotonically to the cap; lose enough people to drop below
  `POPULATION_CRITICAL_THRESHOLD` (4) and reproduction (colocated pair,
  affinity ≥ 0.6, both mature/healthy) can dead-end permanently, with
  the 0.003/tick migrant roll as the only exit.
- On the user's live runs the funnel is *worse* than these probes, for
  two reasons: (a) a real LLM's goal choices are less survival-tuned
  than `fallback_goal`'s hard `hunger > 0.6 → forage` rule (though an
  adversarial probe that never picked FORAGE below the 0.9 critical
  override *still* grew to cap — see 0.1a); (b) at pop ≳ 50 the LLM
  queue backlogs (see §3.6) and goal decisions arrive sim-days stale.
- **An old-age collapse wave does not materialize**: the initial cohort
  (lifespans 20k–40k ticks, all spawned at age 0) dies across a 20k-tick
  window while births continuously backfill at the cap.

**Fix recommendation (priority: High):** either start worlds in March,
or spawn the initial population clustered with one seeded food cache —
and treat the deeper issue as §4.1 (the sim has no carrying-capacity
mechanic, so once past the funnel nothing ever pushes back).

#### 0.1a Food is over-abundant after the funnel — measured

At tick 22k–30k both seeds show **800–1,000 simultaneously-READY farm
plots** for ≤200 people, plus 200–320 standing buildings. Farming costs
nothing (auto-planted at 0.01/tick per occupied grassland tile, no
labor, no upkeep, plots persist until eaten), so the map carpets itself
in food. An adversarial goal policy (agents never deliberately seek
food below the 0.9 emergency override) produced the *same* growth to
cap — i.e. agent decisions currently cannot fail at survival. Scarcity,
the stated point of half the deterministic systems, is effectively off
after year one.

### 0.2 Do town-brain decisions actually influence the world?

**Yes — real and measured, but narrow, and with three caveats.**

- Measured effect on `choose_building_kind` (4,000 draws each):
  priority `food` moves granary share **23% → 43%**; `growth`/`defense`
  move hut share 42% → 64%; `health` moves hospital 8% → 17%. Plus the
  whether-to-build multipliers (×1.6 on-priority / ×0.7 off).
- **Caveat 1 — the lever only touches construction.** "food" priority
  does not change foraging, farming, granary stocking, or goal
  assignment; "defense" boosts huts (there is no defense mechanic);
  "commerce"/"education" only reweight one building draw that fires a
  few times per year. The town brain steers perhaps 1% of what happens
  per tick.
- **Caveat 2 — verified degenerate equilibrium in fallback mode:** the
  fallback priority locked to `food` for the entire 30k ticks in both
  seeds. Root cause: its granary-fill test divides by
  `granary_capacity`, which grows with every granary built, so the
  ratio stays < 0.2 essentially forever. A live LLM will vary more, but
  the fallback — which also runs on every timeout — is a one-note voice.
- **Caveat 3 — player whispers are consumed even when the LLM call
  fails.** `_maybe_schedule_town_brain` clears `player_influence` at
  schedule time; on timeout/fallback the whisper is silently discarded
  (the fallback ignores whispers entirely). A player's nudge can vanish
  without ever being read. Fix: only clear on a non-fallback resolution,
  or re-queue on fallback.

## 1. Architecture

### 1.1 The real God object is `Settlement` (Critical, compounds)

`Population` (1,720 lines) and `SimulationEngine` (1,103) are large but
mostly *wide*; `Settlement` is the one that's structurally dangerous:
a world-singleton that has accreted buildings, vehicles, materials,
currency, education, era, name, traditions, inventions, festivals,
beliefs, priority(+history), player influence, temperament, omen
history, and player standing — ~25 fields spanning four conceptually
separate domains (physical plant, economy, culture, Phase G). Every
release adds fields here, and every field added makes the acknowledged
"multiple named settlements" refactor strictly harder. **Recommend:
before attempting multi-settlement, split Settlement in place into
composed sub-objects (`Infrastructure`, `Economy`, `Culture`,
`Disposition`) with unchanged serialization — a pure-refactor batch
that shrinks the eventual singleton-breaking change to "instantiate N
of these" instead of "untangle 25 fields under pressure."** Priority:
the next dedicated session, before more fields land.

### 1.2 Ten hand-rolled copies of the same LLM job (High)

`SimulationEngine` contains ten `_maybe_schedule_X` / `_run_X` pairs
(naming, chronicle, documentary, tradition, invention, festival,
town_brain, beliefs, omen, dialogue) that differ only in: cadence gate,
prompt builder, fallback, and apply-function. This is the single
biggest missing extension point: a declarative `LlmJob(cadence,
build_prompt, fallback, apply)` table would delete ~400 lines, make new
jobs data instead of code, and give one place to add the backpressure
§3.6 needs. Priority: High — every future LLM feature pays this tax.

### 1.3 Parameter-threading instead of a tick context (Medium)

`Population.tick` takes 11 parameters; adding any subsystem widens
signatures through `World.tick` → `Population.tick` → helpers. A single
`TickContext` (terrain, resources, settlement, farms, wildlife, roads,
weather, night, rng) would stop the arity creep.

### 1.4 Stringly-typed events with scattered category registries (Medium)

Events are `(category, description)` tuples, and at least four
independent frozensets/dicts must agree on category names:
`_GOOD/_ILL_FORTUNE_CATEGORIES` (buildings.py), `HISTORY_CATEGORIES`
(snapshot.py), `_TERRAIN_CHANGING_CATEGORIES` (engine.py), and the UI's
`categoryMeta`. Adding an event category silently misses consumers.
Recommend one `EventKind` registry with tags (`fortune=+1`,
`history=True`, `terrain=True`, icon) that all four derive from.

### 1.5 Persistence will kill a long-running world (Critical)

- **`snapshots` is append-only full-world JSON every 60 ticks, never
  pruned.** At 1 tick/s that's one multi-hundred-KB row per minute,
  forever — GBs per real week, and `load_latest_snapshot` only ever
  reads the newest row. The "rewind for free" rationale doesn't hold:
  nothing reads old rows. Fix: keep last K + sparse keyframes, or
  `DELETE` older than N on save.
- **`log_event` commits per event** — dozens of fsyncs on a busy tick
  even in WAL mode. Batch: commit once per tick.
- `events` has no index on `category`; `history_events` and the
  monthly `recent_events` prompt-feed scans grow linearly with world
  age. Add `idx_events_category` (or a composite with id).

### 1.6 What's architecturally *right* (preserve)

- Single-writer tick loop with queued interventions, and the pause/speed
  bypass rationale — textbook correct.
- Every LLM call has a non-blocking deterministic fallback (liveness
  contract). Never weaken this.
- Objective state vs. subjective belief as separate stores.
- Constants-with-why-docstrings convention; decision log discipline.
- `World.to_dict`/`from_dict` with subsystem backfill.

## 2. Emergence

The stated priority is emergence, but the systems most likely to
generate it are currently **open loops** — they narrate but don't feed
back:

1. **Culture has no mechanical teeth.** Traditions, inventions (beyond
   +0.15 work each), festivals-as-text, chronicles, documentaries, and
   rumors change *no behavior*. A rumor is a memory string; a tradition
   is a prompt garnish. The single highest-leverage emergence change
   that isn't §4.1: give each new tradition/invention a small,
   LLM-chosen mechanical rider from a fixed menu (e.g. tradition →
   +boost to autumn festivals / winter granary discipline / grief
   recovery; the LLM picks which lever, the engine applies a bounded
   constant). Culture then compounds: beliefs → dialogue → relationships
   → who builds together → what stands → what the chronicle says.
2. **Rumors should move third-party opinion.** `apply_dialogue` already
   receives rumor text; resolve rumor subjects against agent names the
   same way beliefs do, and nudge the *listeners'* relationship/trust
   toward the subject. Gossip becomes a real social force with ~30
   lines, reusing `resolve_subject_agent_id`.
3. **Agent goals barely matter (measured).** Foraging, construction,
   granary stocking, trade, and repair all fire from colocation
   regardless of goal; goals only bias movement targets. An adversarial
   goal policy changed nothing. Either give goals exclusive gates (a
   GATHER agent doesn't harvest-stock; a SOCIALIZE agent doesn't repair)
   or accept that per-agent LLM cognition is currently flavor — the
   honest fix is the former.
4. **Construction siting is pure chance** while *what* is steered —
   where things get built is the most visible long-run map signature.
   Cheap version: bias site choice toward road-adjacent/granary-adjacent
   tiles; LLM version: monthly town-brain picks a district hint.
5. **Boom-bust is fenced off** by `POPULATION_CAP` — see §4.1.

## 3. LLM cognition

1. **Memory is one sentence deep.** `Agent.memories` caps at 8 strings
   FIFO and only `memories[-1]` reaches the cognition prompt. An
   agent's whole inner life at decision time is its latest memory. Cheap
   fix (no new state): include the 3 most recent memories, and never
   evict a memory that mentions a parent/child death (salience by kind
   tag, not embedding).
2. **Cognition prompt lacks the decision-relevant facts.** The agent
   chooses forage/socialize/gather without being told whether food is
   near, who's colocated, or what its current goal is. Add
   nearest-food-distance, colocated names, and current goal — three
   lines of prompt, direct quality win on a 2B model.
3. **Belief revision by list index is fragile for a 2B model.** The LLM
   returns `"revises": <int>` into the prompt's enumeration; an
   off-by-one hallucination silently overwrites the wrong theory (parse
   only bounds-checks). Match on subject string instead, falling back to
   append.
4. **Beliefs are fed ground truth, so they can't be wrong.** The whole
   point of subjective belief is divergence, but the beliefs prompt
   receives accurate stats and accurate event descriptions. Feed it only
   the event *narrations* plus its own prior beliefs (no stat block) and
   the town's theories will drift, get corrected, and occasionally stay
   wrong — which is the design goal stated in CLAUDE.md.
5. **Dialogue leaves no memory.** Two agents can't reference their last
   conversation (only rumors/bond-crossings are remembered). Store a
   one-line `talked with X about Y` memory on surfaced exchanges.
6. **No backpressure between scheduling and throughput (Critical in
   live mode).** Sustained scheduling is up to 3 dialogue + several
   cognition jobs per tick; measured live throughput on the target
   hardware is ~1 call / 17–20 s ≈ 0.05–0.2 completions per tick.
   The semaphore queues everything: the asyncio task backlog grows
   without bound (memory), and results are applied sim-days stale
   (a goal computed from a 9-days-ago hunger snapshot). Fixes, in
   order: (a) cap pending jobs and *skip scheduling* when the backlog
   exceeds ~2× `llm_max_concurrent`; (b) tag each job with its schedule
   tick and drop results older than N ticks at apply time; (c) prefer
   triggered cognition over routine daily slots when rationing.
7. **Prompt token growth over years.** `traditions`, `inventions`,
   `festivals` lists grow unbounded and several go whole into prompts;
   a multi-year world will slow every call and drown the 2B model.
   Cap what's *sent* (last 3 + count), even if the full lists persist.
8. **Model choice (8 GB RAM, CPU):** stay on `qwen3.5:2b` with
   `think:false` — it's user-verified, and measured p95 ≈ 20 s means a
   4B model would roughly double latency and worsen §3.6. The documented
   size-up path (`qwen3:4b`) is right *only if* dialogue quality is the
   complaint and concurrency is dropped to 2. Do not raise
   `llm_max_concurrent` above 4 on CPU; consider lowering to 2 — Ollama
   parallel CPU requests share threads, and 2 streams at ~2× speed beat
   4 at ~4× latency for staleness.

## 4. Deterministic simulation

1. **No carrying capacity — the cap is doing ecology's job (Critical
   for the project's purpose).** Reproduction checks current hunger
   only; farming is free and unbounded; so population rockets to the
   hard cap and stays. Replace with food-driven limits: farms need a
   planting *act* (goal-gated) and decay untended; reproduction requires
   personal/granary food surplus, not just non-starvation. Demography
   then couples to weather, disasters, labor allocation, and town-brain
   priorities through one real constraint — the single change that would
   most increase emergence.
2. **Huts do nothing.** No housing/shelter mechanic exists, yet weather
   harshness and night costs do. Make resting in a standing building
   negate the harsh-weather multiplier and huts define comfortable
   capacity — buildings acquire demand, "growth" priority acquires
   meaning, and storms acquire consequences.
3. **Economy is sink-starved.** Currency (cap 50) has one sink
   (emergency rations); materials (cap 30) stops binding once GATHER
   supply exceeds building starts. Trade v1 is altruistic food-sharing,
   not exchange. Lowest-effort fixes: building *upkeep* draws currency;
   repairs draw materials.
4. **Death is a cliff** at `max_age_ticks`; no age-graded frailty. An
   elder is identical to an adult until the tick they die. Small
   energy-recovery decay after ~0.8×max_age would make "the village is
   aging" visible in behavior, not just the overlay.
5. Weather/disasters/wildlife/roads/terrain-evolution are individually
   sound and properly interlocked (thresholds retuned against realized
   EMA ranges — good discipline). Weather is spatially uniform; fine at
   64×64, revisit if world size grows.

## 5. UI / UX (observatory lens)

The map-primary direction and two-surface split are right and already
real. The biggest gap for an *observatory* is time: the UI shows only
"now" plus an event feed.

1. **No time-series anywhere (High).** Population-over-time is the
   single most informative civilization chart and it's invisible.
   Client-side ring buffer + 3 sparklines (population, granary food,
   materials) in the details drawer — zero server changes.
2. **Social geography is invisible on the map.** The relationship graph
   lives in a separate panel; draw faint bond/rivalry arcs between
   colocated agents on the map itself at high zoom (or on hover of an
   agent, show their bonds as map lines) — the observatory reading of
   "who lives near whom."
3. **Belief/priority changes have no visual diff** — the History tab
   shows formations, but the Town Brain panel could mark which theory
   changed since last month (one ✦ glyph).
4. Name collisions (§6.3) will eventually make the NPC inspector
   ambiguous too — surface agent id subtly in the inspector header.
5. Keep the dev console as the only home of prompts/timings — correct
   split, don't leak it.

## 6. Performance

Measured (this environment, LLM off):

| population | ms/tick |
|-----------:|--------:|
| 12         | 1.3     |
| 50         | 1.8     |
| 200        | 7.9     |
| 500        | 45.9    |

1. **O(N²) rival-scan dominates (High).** The per-agent rival-tile set
   comprehension in `_dispatch_movement` scans the full position
   snapshot for every agent (40% of population-tick time at N=200; the
   superlinear term above). Fix: build one shared `id → (x,y)` dict per
   tick and precompute each agent's rival set only for agents that
   *have* any relationship ≤ threshold (rare). 10× population (2,000)
   without this ≈ 700+ ms/tick — over the tick budget on the target
   hardware once the LLM contends for CPU.
2. `_nearest_ready_farm` / `_nearest_stocked_granary` / `_nearest_
   resource` are linear scans per seeking agent per tick — fine at
   hundreds of plots, quadratic-ish at the measured ~1,000 ready plots
   × 200 agents. A coarse spatial bucket (16×16 cells) fixes all three.
3. `World._tick_disasters` rebuilds the full water-tile set every tick
   (64×64 scan); water changes only on logged lake/flood events — cache
   and invalidate on those events. Same for `Settlement.at`'s linear
   scan (called several times per agent per tick): keep a `(x,y) →
   Building` dict alongside the list.
4. **Memory:** no unbounded in-process growth found (cooldown dicts are
   pruned, LLM debug is last-per-job, task set discards) **except** the
   live-mode LLM task backlog (§3.6) — that is the leak the user's
   overnight runs will hit. On disk, §1.5 is the real leak.
5. **C/C++ is not warranted.** The hot loops are dict-and-branch logic,
   not numeric kernels; the wins above are algorithmic (10–50×
   available) and keep the stdlib-only, hackable character. If a 10×
   world *and* 10× population is ever a hard requirement, the movement/
   needs inner loop is the one candidate worth vectorizing (numpy) or
   compiling (Cython/mypyc — same code, ~5–20×) before reaching for C.
   A C rewrite would also forfeit the project's actual bottleneck fix:
   LLM latency, which no compiled tick loop helps.
6. Decades of history: bounded in RAM (agent memories capped, histories
   capped) but unbounded in prompts (§3.7) and on disk (§1.5).

## 7. Scientific value

The platform is one instrumentation layer away from being genuinely
useful for artificial-society work:

1. **Metrics harness first:** a `--metrics-csv` mode writing one row
   per N ticks (population, births/deaths by cause, mean/gini personal
   food, mean±sd relationship, bond/rivalry counts, rumor count, belief
   count/mean confidence, priority, temperament) plus run-id and config
   hash. Everything below depends on it.
2. **Ablations the design begs for:** LLM vs fallback (does the LLM
   measurably change macro outcomes? — currently unknown and, per §2.3,
   plausibly "no"); `phase_g_intensity` 0 vs 1; dialogue on/off vs
   relationship-network topology.
3. **Rumor epidemiology:** seed one traceable rumor via an intervention
   and log which agents' memories carry it per tick — spread curves vs
   trust topology, believers vs skeptics (the trust lever already
   creates the two classes).
4. **Belief-accuracy drift:** score each settlement belief against
   ground truth (many are checkable: "the harvests are failing" vs farm
   yields) and plot accuracy over time — a direct measure of the
   "subjective vs objective" design goal, and it gets more interesting
   after §3.4.
5. **Replay substrate exists** (append-only events + snapshots) but
   needs the snapshot pruning redesign (§1.5) to add keyframed scrubbing
   without an unbounded DB.

## 8. Failure analysis — where it breaks first over simulated years

| Failure | Severity | When it bites | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| DB grows without bound (snapshots) | Critical | Real weeks | Prune/keyframe (§1.5) |
| LLM task backlog + stale results | Critical | Live runs at pop ≳ 50 | Backpressure (§3.6) |
| Stagnation at POPULATION_CAP, food post-scarcity | High | Sim-year 1+ | Carrying capacity (§4.1) |
| Name collisions break belief→person resolution | High | Pop ≳ ~40 | Uniquify at birth (pool draw minus living names) |
| Town-brain fallback locked on "food" | Medium | Any fallback-heavy run | Fix granary-ratio test; vary fallback |
| Whispers lost on LLM fallback | Medium | Timeout months | Clear only on success |
| Prompt bloat from unbounded culture lists | Medium | Sim-year 2+ | Cap what's sent (§3.7) |
| O(N²) movement scan | Medium | Pop ≳ 500 | Shared position map (§6.1) |
| Event-category registry drift | Medium | Every new event kind | Typed registry (§1.4) |
| Repetitive narrative (fallback pools, one-note priorities) | Low | Fallback-heavy runs | More pools; §0.2 caveat 2 |

## 9. Direct answers

**Three biggest architectural risks**
1. `Settlement` as an ever-fattening singleton — every release raises
   the cost of the one refactor the roadmap says is next.
2. No LLM scheduling backpressure — live-mode memory growth and
   sim-days-stale decisions, invisible until an overnight run.
3. Unbounded persistence (snapshots, per-event commits, prompt-fed
   unbounded lists) — the "runs forever" promise breaks on disk and in
   tokens before it breaks in RAM.

**Three biggest missed opportunities**
1. Food-driven carrying capacity instead of a hard cap — turns half the
   existing systems into one interacting ecology.
2. Culture with mechanical teeth (traditions/rumors/beliefs changing
   small real constants) — the emergence flywheel the design promises.
3. A metrics/A-B harness — the project cannot currently answer "did the
   LLM matter?", which is both its scientific and its design question.

**Design decisions to preserve absolutely**
Single-writer tick loop + queued interventions; fallback-on-every-LLM-
call liveness; objective/subjective state separation; the ambiguity
discipline of Phase G; constants-with-rationale and the decision log;
snapshot backfill migration; the two-surface UI split.

**Assumptions to challenge before future development**
That `POPULATION_CAP` is a safety valve (it is the ecology); that
presence-driven colocation is the right trigger for *every* mechanic
(it makes goals irrelevant); that per-agent LLM goals meaningfully
steer outcomes (measured: they don't yet); that huts need no function;
that snapshots-forever is cheap; that more `llm_max_concurrent` is more
throughput on CPU.

**If redesigning from first principles**
Keep: Python, SQLite, local-LLM-with-fallback, single-writer loop.
Change: (1) typed event bus that every system publishes to and
temperament/history/UI/beliefs all consume from one registry; (2) a
declarative LLM-job framework with cadence, budget, and staleness
policy per job; (3) state split into entity stores (settlement never a
singleton, agents in an indexed store); (4) needs-based economy (food +
shelter as coupled constraints) instead of caps; (5) metrics emission
as a first-class system from day one.

**The single change that would most increase long-term emergence**
Replace the hard population cap and free farming with a food-driven
carrying capacity (farming requires a deliberate act and decays
untended; reproduction requires surplus). Every existing system —
weather, seasons, disasters, wildlife, granaries, trade, town brain,
migration, even Phase G's fortune-reading — already touches food;
making food actually bind couples them all through one constraint and
produces boom-bust, migration pressure, and scarcity politics nobody
has to script.
